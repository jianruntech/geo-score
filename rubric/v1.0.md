# AIV Rubric v1.0

> **AIV (AI Visibility) Score** — a 0–100 measure of whether AI answer engines can
> reach, parse, trust and cite a website.
>
> This document is the complete, versioned scoring specification. Anyone can implement it.
> Scores are only comparable when the rubric version matches.

**Status:** stable · **Released:** 2026-09-08 · **Checks:** 28 · **Max:** 100

## Weights

| # | Pillar | Points | The question it answers |
|:-:|---|:-:|---|
| 1 | Infrastructure | 20 | Can AI crawlers reach and read the site at all? |
| 2 | Structured Data | 20 | Can a machine understand what each page *is*? |
| 3 | Content Citability | **25** | Is the content in a shape an engine will quote? |
| 4 | Brand Authority | 20 | Does anything outside your own domain corroborate you? |
| 5 | Platform Visibility | 15 | Do the engines actually surface you today? |

Content Citability carries the largest weight because retrieval-augmented answer
engines select *passages*, not domains. A site can rank poorly in classic search
and still be cited if its passages are extractable — and the reverse is also true.

## Pillar 1 · Infrastructure — 20 points

| Check | Pts | Passes when |
|---|:-:|---|
| AI crawlers allowed in `robots.txt` | 4 | The named AI user-agents are not disallowed (see [ai-crawlers.md](../reference/ai-crawlers.md)). Partial credit by proportion allowed |
| `llms.txt` present and complete | 4 | Resolves at `/llms.txt`, is valid Markdown, has an H1, a summary blockquote, and at least 3 sectioned link groups |
| `llms-full.txt` present | 3 | Resolves and is substantially longer than `llms.txt` |
| `ai.txt` policy file | 2 | Resolves at `/ai.txt` |
| Sitemap completeness | 3 | `sitemap.xml` resolves, is referenced from `robots.txt`, and covers all indexable locales |
| GEO `<link>` tags in `<head>` | 2 | `rel="llms"`, `rel="llms-full"`, or `rel="ai-policy"` present |
| Crawl performance | 2 | Server-rendered HTML contains the primary content without JavaScript execution |

## Pillar 2 · Structured Data — 20 points

| Check | Pts | Passes when |
|---|:-:|---|
| `Organization` + `WebSite` sitewide | 4 | Valid JSON-LD on every indexable page |
| `Article`/`BlogPosting` with author | 4 | Present on article pages, with a named `author` that has a resolvable identity |
| `FAQPage` on Q&A pages | 3 | Present wherever the page is structured as questions |
| `speakable` coverage | 3 | Declared on pages with a clear answer passage |
| `Product`/`Offer` on commercial pages | 3 | Present on pricing or product pages |
| `HowTo` / `BreadcrumbList` | 3 | Present where the page is a procedure or sits in a hierarchy |

## Pillar 3 · Content Citability — 25 points

| Check | Pts | Passes when |
|---|:-:|---|
| Answer passages on key pages | 5 | Each key page opens its main section with a self-contained 40–90 word passage that answers the page's question without needing surrounding context |
| Statistics carry a source | 5 | Numeric claims are attributed to a named, linkable source |
| Named expert authorship | 5 | Author is a real, identifiable person with a bio, not "admin" or a brand name |
| Questions match natural phrasing | 5 | Headings are phrased the way a person would ask an assistant, not as keyword strings |
| Freshness signals | 5 | `dateModified` present and within the last 180 days on evergreen pages |

## Pillar 4 · Brand Authority — 20 points

| Check | Pts | Passes when |
|---|:-:|---|
| Knowledge-graph presence | 5 | A Wikidata item or equivalent authority record exists and is referenced via `sameAs` |
| Third-party listings | 5 | Present on at least one independent review or directory site |
| Video presence | 4 | A channel with published content exists and is linked via `sameAs` |
| Independent mentions | 3 | The brand is discussed on sites you do not control |
| `sameAs` links resolve | 3 | Every `sameAs` URL returns 200 and the destination references the brand back |

## Pillar 5 · Platform Visibility — 15 points

| Check | Pts | Passes when |
|---|:-:|---|
| Search Console verified | 3 | Property verified |
| Bing Webmaster submitted | 3 | Site submitted and indexed |
| Multi-engine query test performed | 3 | A fixed query set has been run against ≥3 engines and the results recorded |
| Answer-shape fit | 3 | Content length and structure match how the target engines format answers |
| Non-English engine coverage | 3 | If the audience is non-English, the corresponding regional engines were tested |

## Score bands

| Score | Band | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| 0–40 | Critical | Engines cannot reliably reach or parse the site. Infrastructure first |
| 41–60 | Below average | Reachable but not structured. Schema and passage shape are the gap |
| 61–75 | Good | Structurally sound. Remaining gap is usually authority and freshness |
| 76–90 | Strong | Cited in practice. Work shifts to per-engine coverage and monitoring |
| 91–100 | Leading | Maintain, expand engine coverage, defend against competitor moves |

## Rules for a valid score

1. **Report the rubric version.** A score without a version is meaningless.
2. **Score the site, not a page.** Page-level checks are sampled across at least
   the homepage, one product/service page, and one article.
3. **Partial credit is allowed** where the check names a proportion; otherwise
   a check is 0 or full.
4. **Record the date.** Engine behaviour changes; a score is a point-in-time reading.
5. **Do not average across rubric versions.** Re-score with the current version instead.

## Changing this rubric

The weights and checks are versioned deliberately so that scores stay comparable.
Proposals to add, remove or reweight a check go through
[a rubric proposal issue](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/rubric_proposal.yml) and must state
what observable signal justifies the change. Cosmetic edits do not bump the version;
any change to weights or to the pass condition of a check does.
