# AIV Rubric v1.0

> **AIV (AI Visibility) Score** — a 0–100 measure of whether AI answer engines can
> reach, parse, trust and cite a website.
>
> This document is the complete, versioned scoring specification. Anyone can implement it.
> Scores are only comparable when the rubric version matches.

**Status:** stable · **Released:** 2026-09-08 · **Checks:** 29 · **Nominal max:** 100
**Effective max varies** — see rule 5. Always report `score / observable max`.

## Weights

| # | Pillar | Points | The question it answers |
|:-:|---|:-:|---|
| 1 | Infrastructure | 20 | Can AI crawlers reach and read the site at all? |
| 2 | Structured Data | 20 | Can a machine understand what each page *is*? |
| 3 | Content Citability | **25** | Is the content in a shape an engine will quote? |
| 4 | Brand Authority | 20 | Does anything outside your own domain corroborate you? |
| 5 | Platform Visibility | 15 | Do the engines actually surface you today? |

Content Citability carries the largest weight because retrieval-augmented answer
engines select *passages*, not domains. A site can rank poorly in classic search
and still be cited if its passages are extractable — and the reverse is also true.

## Pillar 1 · Infrastructure — 20 points

| Check | Pts | Credit | Passes when |
|---|:-:|:-:|---|
| AI crawlers allowed in `robots.txt` | 3 | prop. | The retrieval user-agents are not disallowed. `floor(3 × allowed / 10)` — see [ai-crawlers.md](../reference/ai-crawlers.md). Opt-out tokens and training crawlers are not counted |
| Retrieval crawlers actually reachable | 3 | prop. | Requesting the homepage with each retrieval user-agent returns **200 with the real page** — not 403, not a challenge page, not a JS interstitial. `floor(3 × reachable / 10)` |
| `llms.txt` present and complete | 3 | all/none | Resolves at `/llms.txt`, valid Markdown, has an H1, a summary blockquote, and ≥3 sectioned link groups |
| `llms-full.txt` present | 2 | all/none | Resolves and is substantially longer than `llms.txt` |
| `ai.txt` policy file | 2 | all/none | Resolves at `/ai.txt` |
| Sitemap completeness | 3 | all/none | `sitemap.xml` resolves, is referenced from `robots.txt`, covers all indexable locales |
| GEO `<link>` tags in `<head>` | 2 | all/none | `rel="llms"`, `rel="llms-full"`, or `rel="ai-policy"` present |
| Content in server-rendered HTML | 2 | all/none | Fetching without executing JavaScript returns the page's primary content |

> **Why reachability is scored separately from `robots.txt`.**
> `robots.txt` states an intention; the edge decides what actually happens. A WAF, bot
> manager or rate limiter can return 403 or a challenge to `OAI-SearchBot` while
> `robots.txt` says `Allow: /`. From an engine's side the site is simply gone.
> This gap widened in September 2026 when Cloudflare began blocking mixed-purpose AI
> crawlers by default for new and free-tier zones — a site can lose AI visibility
> without anyone changing a line of its own configuration.

## Pillar 2 · Structured Data — 20 points

| Check | Pts | Credit | Passes when |
|---|:-:|:-:|---|
| `Organization` + `WebSite` sitewide | 4 | all/none | Valid JSON-LD on every indexable page |
| `Article`/`BlogPosting` with author | 4 | prop. | Present on article pages, with a named `author` that has a resolvable identity |
| `FAQPage` on Q&A pages | 3 | prop. | Present wherever the page is structured as questions |
| `speakable` coverage | 3 | prop. | Declared on pages with a clear answer passage |
| `Product`/`Offer` on commercial pages | 3 | all/none | Present on pricing or product pages |
| `HowTo` / `BreadcrumbList` | 3 | all/none | Present where the page is a procedure or sits in a hierarchy |

## Pillar 3 · Content Citability — 25 points

| Check | Pts | Credit | Passes when |
|---|:-:|:-:|---|
| Answer passages on key pages | 5 | prop. | Each key page opens its main section with a self-contained 40–90 word passage that answers the page's question without needing surrounding context |
| Statistics carry a source | 5 | prop. | Numeric claims are attributed to a named, linkable source |
| Named expert authorship | 5 | prop. | Author is a real, identifiable person with a bio, not "admin" or a brand name |
| Questions match natural phrasing | 5 | prop. | Headings are phrased the way a person would ask an assistant, not as keyword strings |
| Freshness signals | 5 | prop. | `dateModified` present and within the last 180 days on evergreen pages |

## Pillar 4 · Brand Authority — 20 points

| Check | Pts | Credit | Passes when |
|---|:-:|:-:|---|
| Knowledge-graph presence | 5 | all/none | A Wikidata item or equivalent authority record exists and is referenced via `sameAs` |
| Third-party listings | 5 | all/none | Present on at least one independent review or directory site |
| Video presence | 4 | all/none | A channel with published content exists and is linked via `sameAs` |
| Independent mentions | 3 | all/none | The brand is discussed on sites you do not control |
| `sameAs` links resolve | 3 | prop. | Every `sameAs` URL returns 200 and the destination references the brand back |

## Pillar 5 · Platform Visibility — 15 points

| Check | Pts | Credit | Passes when |
|---|:-:|:-:|---|
| Search Console verified | 3 | all/none | Property verified |
| Bing Webmaster submitted | 3 | all/none | Site submitted and indexed |
| Cited in a multi-engine query test | 3 | prop. | A fixed set of ≥10 buyer-intent queries is run against ≥3 engines. Score `floor(3 × engines-where-cited / 3)`. Running the test but never being cited scores **0** — this check measures the outcome, not the procedure |
| Answer-shape fit | 3 | prop. | Content length and structure match how the target engines format answers |
| Non-English engine coverage | 3 | all/none | If the audience is non-English, the corresponding regional engines were tested |

## Score bands

| Score | Band | What it means |
|:-:|---|---|
| 0–40 | Critical | Engines cannot reliably reach or parse the site. Infrastructure first |
| 41–60 | Below average | Reachable but not structured. Schema and passage shape are the gap |
| 61–75 | Good | Structurally sound. Remaining gap is usually authority and freshness |
| 76–90 | Strong | Cited in practice. Work shifts to per-engine coverage and monitoring |
| 91–100 | Leading | Maintain, expand engine coverage, defend against competitor moves |

## Rules for a valid score

**1 · Report the rubric version.** `AIV 62 (v1.0)` is a claim. `AIV 62` is not.

**2 · Report the date.** Engine behaviour moves; a score is a point-in-time reading.

**3 · Sampling is fixed, and the URLs are published.** Score the site, not a page.
Sample exactly **8 URLs**: the homepage, the 3 most recently modified product/service
pages from `sitemap.xml`, and the 4 most recently modified articles. Where a site has
fewer of a type, take what exists and say so. **The report must list every sampled URL** —
without them the score cannot be reproduced. For checks that read "on key pages", the
denominator is this sample.

**4 · Credit is per the Credit column.**
- `prop.` — `floor(points × meeting / total)`. **Always round down.**
- `all/none` — 0 or full. No judgement calls in between.

**5 · Three states, not two.** Every check is `scored`, `failed`, or `unobservable`.

- **failed** — the check applies and the site does not meet it. Scores 0, stays in the denominator.
- **unobservable** — you could not verify it from where you stand. Common cases:
  Search Console and Bing verification when auditing someone else's site; any check
  requiring credentials. **Remove those points from the denominator**, do not score 0.
- **not applicable** — the page type does not exist on this site. A brand site with no
  articles, no products and no procedures cannot earn `Article`, `Product/Offer` or
  `HowTo`. **Remove those points from the denominator.** Site type is not a defect.

Report the result as **`AIV <score> / <observable max> (v1.0)`**, e.g. `AIV 48 / 87 (v1.0)`,
and list which checks were excluded and why. **Two scores are comparable only when their
observable maximums match.** A site owner's self-audit and a consultant's external audit
of the same site will usually have different maximums — that is expected, and it is why
the maximum is printed.

**6 · Non-English engines.** If the audience is English-speaking, the non-English check
is `not applicable` and its 3 points leave the denominator. It is never awarded by default.

**7 · Band on the normalised percentage.** With a variable denominator, the band is
read from `score / observable max × 100`, not from the raw score. Print both:
`AIV 33 / 88 (v1.0) · normalised 38% · Critical`.

**8 · Do not average across rubric versions**, and do not plot them on one axis.
Re-score with the current version instead.
## Changing this rubric

The weights and checks are versioned deliberately so that scores stay comparable.
Proposals to add, remove or reweight a check go through
[a rubric proposal issue](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/rubric_proposal.yml) and must state
what observable signal justifies the change. Cosmetic edits do not bump the version;
any change to weights or to the pass condition of a check does.
