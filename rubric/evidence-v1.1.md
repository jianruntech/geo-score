# Evidence basis · AIV rubric v1.1

*简体中文：[evidence-v1.1.zh-CN.md](evidence-v1.1.zh-CN.md)*

> **Metadata only.** This page changes no weight, tier or score in [v1.1](v1.1.md). It records
> what each check's weight rests on, so a reader can judge the weight for themselves. The same
> data is the `evidence` object on every check in [v1.1.json](v1.1.json).

Every check below says whether its weight rests on published research, on an engine or crawler
operator's own documentation, on what we saw auditing real sites, or on a convention nobody has
shown to matter. Where the evidence is thin or points the other way, the note says so.

## What the four bases mean

| Basis | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `research` | A published study measured the effect, or the mechanism the check relies on. Preprints and industry studies are named as such in the note — most of the research here is not peer-reviewed. |
| `vendor-doc` | The operator of an engine or crawler documents the mechanism in its own documentation. For most checks that operator is Google, and Google's documentation covers Google Search, not every answer engine. |
| `field-observation` | No external source supports the weight. It rests on what the rubric authors saw auditing real sites — the [calibration record](calibration-v1.1.md) and the [benchmark](../benchmark/README.md) — and on their judgement. |
| `convention` | The check measures adoption of a published proposal or de-facto convention that no major answer engine has confirmed using for answers. The points reward adoption, not a demonstrated effect. |

A basis says where the support comes from, not how strong it is — the note says that. Every source
was fetched, and checked for the claim it is cited for, on the date in the *Verified* column. Where a vendor's documentation argues
against a check, it is listed as a source too.

**Counts.** Of the 21 scored checks, 11 rest on `vendor-doc` (45 points), 7 on `research` (39 points),
2 on `field-observation` (11 points) and 1 on `convention` (5 points). Of the 4 bonus checks, 3 are
`convention` and 1 is `vendor-doc`.

## Where the evidence is thinnest

Relative to the points each carries:

- **`p2.answer-passages` (9)** — the heaviest check rests on two preprints, one of them using simulated
  engines, and Google says content need not be chunked for its AI features.
- **`p2.question-intent` (7)** — no external source; Google's guidance and one observational preprint
  point the other way.
- **`p1.llms-txt` (5)** — a convention on 2.13% of desktop sites that no major answer engine has
  confirmed using, and that Google says Google Search ignores.
- **`p1.page-type` (4)** — of the types the rubric names, only Product still has a Google Search feature
  behind it.
- **`p3.knowledge-graph` (4)** — all-or-nothing, and the "strongest single signal" claim in its rationale
  is not established by any source we found.
- **`p4.question-coverage` (4)** — rests on the auditor's choice of ten questions.

These are candidates for re-weighting in a future version. Any change to a weight ships as a new
rubric version, never as an edit to v1.1 — see [the rubric README](README.md).

## Notes on sources

- **Adoption figures.** The llms.txt figure (2.13% of desktop sites) is from the Web Almanac **2025**
  SEO chapter. The structured-data figures the calibration record quotes alongside it (BreadcrumbList
  5.66%, Product 0.77%, FAQPage about 0.34%) are from the Web Almanac **2024** structured-data chapter;
  the 2025 edition has no structured-data chapter.
- **Preprints.** Wu et al. 2025 (arXiv 2510.11438) uses engines simulated with small commercial models,
  not deployed products. Zhang et al. 2026 (arXiv 2604.25707) is observational and states that it makes
  no causal claims. Kumar 2026 (arXiv 2606.20065) is a measurement study by a GEO vendor. Chen et al.
  2025 (arXiv 2509.08919) and Fang et al. 2025 (arXiv 2509.11353) are preprints. Only Aggarwal et al.
  (arXiv 2311.09735) is cited here with a peer-reviewed venue, KDD 2024.
- **Re-verification.** Vendor documentation in this area changes often: Google removed FAQ rich results
  and published its generative-AI guidance in 2026, and Cloudflare changes its bot defaults on
  15 September 2026. A source is only as current as its *Verified* date.
