# Proposal: lower the weight of `p1.llms-txt` (rubric v1.2, draft)

*简体中文：[v1.2-llms-txt-weight.zh-CN.md](v1.2-llms-txt-weight.zh-CN.md)*

> **Status: draft proposal, not adopted.** Rubric v1.1 and every published score are unchanged, and so is the
> tool. Proposed by Jianrun Tech, the rubric's maintainer, on 2026-11-27. This page is the text of step 1 of
> [the rubric governance](../governance.md#how-a-change-is-made) (a rubric proposal) and, if the maintainers
> accept it, the brief for step 2 (the draft pull request with the v1.2 files).
>
> **Comment period.** A changed weight is a minor version (v1.1 to v1.2), so the draft gets at least **30 days**
> of public comment, counted from the day its pull request opens. It has not opened yet; the dates go here when it
> does. Every objection gets an answer in that pull request, and an independent reviewer reads the draft and its

## Conflict of interest

This proposal is ours, and the governance page asks us to say so. Jianrun Tech sells GEO services: diagnosing
sites and changing them so that they score better. Publishing an llms.txt file is one of the changes such work can
make, so lowering this check's weight lowers what that change is worth on the score, for our clients as for anyone.

## The change

The check keeps its id, what it observes and how: an llms.txt at the site root, whether it returns 200, whether it
carries a site definition passage, and whether it has two or more topic sections that contain links. Only its
points change. The bonus checks `b.llms-full` (2) and `b.geo-link` (1) stay as they are: bonus points sit outside
the denominator and never count against a site that skips them.

**Option A (proposed): 5 points to 2.**

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | absent |
| 1 | present and returns 200 |
| 2 | carries a site definition passage and has 2+ topic sections that contain links |

The three freed points stay in the Understandable pillar (22 points), shared among its other four checks in
proportion to their current points, rounded by largest remainder: `p1.sitemap` 4 to 5, `p1.organization` 6 to 7,
`p1.breadcrumb` 3 (unchanged), `p1.page-type` 4 to 5. Their tier tables would be rescaled in the draft and shown in
its calibration record.

**Option B (for comment): out of the core, into the bonus.** `p1.llms-txt` is retired as a core check (an id is
retired, never reused) and a bonus check, `b.llms-txt`, 1 point, is met by an llms.txt that carries a site
definition passage and two or more linked topic sections. The five freed points are shared the same way:
`p1.sitemap` 5, `p1.organization` 8, `p1.breadcrumb` 4, `p1.page-type` 5. The bonus cap stays at +6.

**Option C: no weight change**, only the evidence table brought up to date.

## Why: the evidence, as of 2026-11-27

The v1.1 evidence table already says the 5 points "reward adoption of a convention, not a demonstrated effect"
([evidence-v1.1.md](../evidence-v1.1.md)). Since it was written (2026-09-27), more has been published, and it points
the same way.

**No major answer engine documents reading it.**

- Google's guide to its generative AI features in Search (published 2026-05-15) has a section on llms.txt and other
  "special" markup and says such AI text files are not needed, "as Google Search itself doesn't use them"; a June
  2026 addition says keeping one for other services is fine
  ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).
- Google's John Mueller compared llms.txt to the keywords meta tag in April 2025
  ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-says-llms-txt-comparable-to-keywords-meta-tag/544804/)),
  wrote in June 2025 that "no AI system currently uses llms.txt"
  ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-llms-txt-39607.html)), and in June 2026 that
  the file cannot be trusted to tell sites apart
  ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-mueller-says-llms-txt-cant-help-llms-differentiate-sites/566027/)).
  A July 2025 report that Google does not support it and is not planning to is second-hand, from a conference
  attendee ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/openai-crawling-llms-txt-files-39811.html)).
- OpenAI's, Perplexity's and Anthropic's crawler documentation does not say their crawlers read llms.txt; the first
  two publish llms.txt files for their own documentation sites, which is not the same thing.

**Most files are not requested.**

- Ahrefs looked at the 137,210 domains with traffic in its Web Analytics in May 2026 and about 38,000 valid root
  llms.txt files among them: 97% received no request that month. Of the requests that were recorded, AI search
  crawlers made about 1.1% (OAI-SearchBot 0.74%), training crawlers 5.3% and SEO audit tools 21.7%
  ([Ahrefs, 2026-06-15](https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/)). Ahrefs calls every figure a ceiling, since its
  customers lean technical. The study does not say how many of those domains had the CDN integration Ahrefs' bot
  analytics needs, so "no request recorded" is the safe reading.
- A panel of 83 sites with server logs over 12 weeks (2026-04-27 to 07-19) saw GPTBot-family agents request
  robots.txt 3,990 times and llms.txt 7 times, ClaudeBot 3,120 and 9, PerplexityBot 775 and 0
  ([EZY Research, 2026-07-27](https://www.ezy.ai/research/do-ai-bots-read-llms-txt)); the publisher sells llms.txt
  generation.
- One site's two months of logs (September and October 2026) counted about 600 requests for llms.txt and
  llms-full.txt; ChatGPT-User made 16,445 requests to the site and one of them was for llms.txt
  ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/which-ai-actually-reads-your-site-two-months-of-llm-traffic-measured)).

**Factor reviews rate it last.** Zyppy's review of 54 published experiments, patents and case studies on what earns
AI citations rated llms.txt 2, last of 23 factors, finding no "credible evidence or experiments" that it influences
citations. The ratings are the author's, by hand, and he says they are not confirmed ranking factors
([Zyppy Signal, 2026-05-07](https://signal.zyppy.com/p/ai-citation-ranking-factors)). A correlation study of about
300,000 domains found no relationship between llms.txt and how often AI engines cite a domain (SE Ranking, as
reported by [Search Engine Journal, 2025-11-20](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/llms-txt-shows-no-clear-effect-on-ai-citations-based-on-300k-domains/561542/);
we have not read the original).

**What points the other way, stated as fairly as we can.**

- Some clients do request it. On seven education sites, OAI-SearchBot requested llms.txt more than 5,000 times
  between 16 and 24 July 2025, then far less
  ([Wix Studio AI Search Lab, 2025-12-18](https://www.wix.com/studio/ai-search-lab/are-ai-bots-crawling-llms-txt-files)).
  Seven days of CDN logs from 25 companies saw ChatGPT account for most llms-full.txt traffic
  ([Mintlify, 2025-06-27](https://www.mintlify.com/blog/how-often-do-llms-visit-llms-txt)). Coding agents such as
  Claude Code fetch it (Evil Martians, above).
- Chrome's Lighthouse added an experimental "Agentic Browsing" category in May 2026 with an llms.txt audit; the
  file is optional there and the category carries no weighted score
  ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/agentic-browsing/llms-txt)).
- A request is not a use, and no study above is a causal experiment. Most were published by vendors with an
  interest, and most identify bots by user-agent alone.
- Adoption is low but rising: 2.13% of desktop sites in the Web Almanac 2025
  ([HTTP Archive](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/seo)).

So the evidence supports a smaller weight in a score of readiness for AI answer engines. It does not show that
llms.txt is useless to every AI client: documentation sites and coding agents are a different question, raised
below.

## What it would move (preliminary)

From [benchmark/checks.csv](../../benchmark/checks.csv): 324 sites, scored with geo-score 1.5.0 on 2026-10-02. The
v1.1 arithmetic (scoring rules 3 to 5) recomputes every published normalised score from that file exactly. Today
137 of the 324 sites get points from `p1.llms-txt` (110 at 5, 26 at 4, 1 at 2), 178 get none, and 9 are not
observed.

| | Scores that move | Range | Band changes | Median score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1.1 | | | | 55 |
| Option A | 201 of 324 | −4 to +4 | 18 | 54 |
| Option B | 224 of 324 | −5 to +6 | 24 | 53.5 |

Sites with llms.txt points mostly lose a point; sites without mostly gain one. This assumes each receiving check's
tier points scale with its new maximum; the draft's calibration record replaces that assumption with real tier
tables and the benchmark re-scored, as governance step 2 requires. A v1.1 score is never recomputed or relabelled.

## How anyone can check this

The check is observed from outside the site, as before. Every source above is public. The table can be recomputed
from benchmark/checks.csv with the arithmetic of [rubric v1.1](../v1.1.md#scoring-rules) and the assumption stated
under it.

## Open questions for the comment period

1. Where the freed points go: shared within the pillar as above, or to a check named for stronger evidence.
2. Option A (a smaller core check) or option B (a bonus that never counts against a site).
3. Whether llms.txt and llms-full.txt belong instead in a separate, future measure of readiness for documentation
   tools and coding agents, where the evidence is better, rather than in a score for answer engines.
4. The sources are re-checked on the day the comment period opens, and any that moved are noted here.
