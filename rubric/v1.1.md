# AIV rubric v1.1

> Supersedes [v1.0](v1.0.md). v1.0 reports stay valid, but the two versions are **not comparable** — what changed and why: [calibration-v1.1.md](calibration-v1.1.md).

## Two scores. Never one

**Readiness** · 100 points — What the site owner can change, observable from outside. This is what the rubric scores, and the range a service can commit to.

**Citation performance** · not out of anything — Whether engines actually cite the site, tested with real industry questions. An outcome — reported separately, never folded in. It is produced by readiness plus time, and asking the same question twice gives different answers, so it is not reproducible.

Folding these together is the most common mistake this kind of rubric makes. It is what made v1.0 label a site with flawless crawler reachability and 60 KB of server-rendered prose *Critical* — a verdict that damages the client and the rubric's own credibility at once.

## Scoring rules

1. **Sample.** Exactly 8 URLs — the homepage, 2 main product or service pages, 2 documentation or knowledge pages, 3 recent content pages. Take all of them if the site has fewer, and say so in the report.
2. **Tiers, not pass/fail.** Take the highest tier the evidence *actually* satisfies, not the one it nearly reaches. This is where discrimination comes from.
3. **Three states**: `scored` / `unobservable` / `not_applicable`. The last two **leave the denominator**. Report as `Readiness X / observable max Y` and band on the normalised percentage.
4. **Gates.** The three `g.` checks score normally *and* cap the total: if any falls short of full marks, readiness caps at 40 and the report leads with it — until a crawler can reach the content, nothing else you change has any effect.
5. **Bonus.** The four `b.` checks stay out of the denominator and only add, capped at +6. These conventions are still barely adopted; doing them is a lead, skipping them is not a penalty.
6. **Substance over format.** A heading matches question intent if a person would phrase their question that way — not if it carries a question mark. A freshness signal is anything that lets a reader or a crawler confirm currency; a schema date and a visible date are equivalent.
7. **Chinese-language sites.** Answer passages are judged at 50–200 characters (English: 25–120 words). This conversion is a convention of this rubric and must be stated in the report's evidence.
8. **Evidence.** Every check needs an observation someone else can reproduce — status code, byte count, the sentence that did or did not qualify. A judgement with no evidence does not stand.

## Bands

| Normalised | Band | Meaning |
|:-:|---|---|
| 83+ | **Leading** | Already ahead. The work shifts from building to holding and widening the lead. |
| 66+ | **Solid** | Foundations are in place. What is left is content depth and brand assets. |
| 51+ | **Growing** | Most of the work has started. What is missing is consistency across the site. |
| 31+ | **Early** | Clear gaps in the basics — most of them are days of work, not quarters. |
| 0+ | **Not started** | Nothing has been built for AI retrieval yet. The largest headroom of any band. |

Thresholds are taken from the AEO ladder published by GW Content, which its own stated distribution backs ("most business sites score between 30 and 55"). Band names describe a **stage, not a verdict** — this is a diagnostic used to decide what to do next.

## This rubric does not tell you how to fix anything

Each check says only **what is measured**, **what the tiers are**, and **why it matters for retrieval**. Fix methods, effort estimates, ownership and repair sequencing are not in this repository — see the scope section of the README. The line is deliberate: a rubric has to publish its criteria to be checkable, and publishing criteria is not the same as publishing an implementation.

## Report shape

- **Ordering**: Findings default to **recoverable points ÷ effort**, not pillar order; unlocking fixes always precede incremental ones.
- **First screen**: The first screen carries three things only: the score and its band, how many points to the next band, and the top 3 findings. The full list goes in an appendix.
- **Every finding**: Every finding carries three parts: why it matters, what is wrong, and what reaching the next tier is worth.

## Reachable · 15 points

### Retrieval crawlers allowed in robots.txt · 5 pts  `g.robots`

The one check where failing makes every other change pointless. If a retrieval crawler is blocked, nothing you do on the page reaches an answer engine.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | robots.txt carries a Disallow that applies to retrieval user-agents |
| 3 | no explicit Disallow, but no explicit Allow either |
| 5 | mainstream retrieval user-agents explicitly allowed |

> Gate check: scored, and also a cap. If any gate falls short of full marks, readiness caps at 40 and the report leads with it — because until a crawler can reach the content, every other change produces no effect.

### Reachable to retrieval user-agents · 5 pts  `g.reachable`

Allowing a crawler in robots.txt is not the same as serving it. CDNs and WAFs block by user-agent, and only a live request shows it.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | most retrieval user-agents are blocked |
| 3 | some are blocked, or the body differs from what a browser receives |
| 5 | all 10 retrieval user-agents return 200 with matching content |

> Gate check: scored, and also a cap. If any gate falls short of full marks, readiness caps at 40 and the report leads with it — because until a crawler can reach the content, every other change produces no effect.

### Main content server-rendered · 5 pts  `g.ssr`

Most retrieval crawlers do not execute JavaScript. Content that appears only in a browser does not exist as far as an answer engine is concerned.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | body copy exists only after JavaScript runs |
| 3 | present on some sampled pages |
| 5 | present in the HTML response on every sampled page |

> Gate check: scored, and also a cap. If any gate falls short of full marks, readiness caps at 40 and the report leads with it — because until a crawler can reach the content, every other change produces no effect.

## Understandable · 22 points

### Sitemap discoverable and fresh · 4 pts  `p1.sitemap`

Retrieval side finds new pages through the sitemap; without lastmod it cannot tell which pages are worth fetching again. A path declared in robots.txt takes precedence over the conventional one.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | cannot be discovered, or does not return 200 |
| 2 | discoverable and returns 200 |
| 4 | and lastmod covers most URLs |

### llms.txt present and structured · 5 pts  `p1.llms-txt`

The only place you get to tell the retrieval side directly who you are and which pages matter. It costs almost nothing.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | absent |
| 2 | present and returns 200 |
| 4 | carries a site definition passage |
| 5 | and has 2+ topic sections that contain links |

### Organization + WebSite sitewide · 6 pts  `p1.organization`

This is how an engine decides which company you are. Without it your brand name can be attributed to someone else who shares it.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | neither present |
| 3 | one of the two present |
| 5 | both present with name, url and logo |
| 6 | and the logo resolves, with sameAs declared |

### BreadcrumbList on nested pages · 3 pts  `p1.breadcrumb`

Tells the retrieval side where a page sits in the site — whether it is an overview or a detail. Excluded if the site has no hierarchy.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | absent |
| 2 | present on some nested pages |
| 3 | present across nested pages |

### Page-type schema where applicable · 4 pts  `p1.page-type`

Product + Offer for commerce, HowTo for docs, FAQPage for Q&A. Excluded entirely if the site has no matching page type.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | absent |
| 2 | present on some page types |
| 4 | present across applicable page types with real field values |

## Content Citability · 35 points

### Self-contained answer passages · 9 pts  `p2.answer-passages`

An engine quotes a passage that stands on its own. Without one, good content still only gets summarised, never cited.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | none on the sampled pages |
| 4 | on a few pages |
| 7 | on half the pages |
| 9 | on most pages |

### Headings match how people ask · 7 pts  `p2.question-intent`

People ask assistants in natural language. Questions (What is X?), tasks (Accept a payment) and explanations (How Connect works) all count; only keyword strings and brand labels do not.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | headings are mostly keyword strings or brand labels |
| 3 | a few headings read like a question someone would ask |
| 5 | about half do |
| 7 | most do |

### Freshness signal present · 6 pts  `p2.freshness`

Engines prefer content whose currency they can confirm. A schema date or a date a reader can see — either one qualifies.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | no date in the page or in structured data |
| 3 | some pages carry a visible date or datePublished |
| 6 | most pages do, and dateModified agrees with the visible date |

### Statistics carry a source · 7 pts  `p2.sourced-stats`

An attributed number is quoted far more readily than a bare one — it gives the citing side something to stand on.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | figures and claims carry no source |
| 3 | some are attributed |
| 5 | most are attributed |
| 7 | most are attributed and the source is clickable and checkable |

### Named, verifiable authorship · 6 pts  `p2.named-author`

A byline that resolves to a person is the core of E-E-A-T. An on-site author page or the person's own homepage both qualify — an on-site bio is not required.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | no byline, or the byline is the organisation |
| 3 | bylined to a real person |
| 6 | and the name links to a verifiable identity page |

## Brand Credibility · 18 points

### Third-party listings · 4 pts  `p3.listings`

Directories, review platforms and app stores are where an engine cross-checks that you exist.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | none |
| 2 | 1–2 |
| 3 | 3–4 |
| 4 | 5 or more |

### Independent mentions · 4 pts  `p3.mentions`

Saying you are good on your own channels is not evidence. Someone else saying it is.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | none |
| 2 | occasional mentions |
| 3 | independent coverage or reviews exist |
| 4 | sustained mentions across channels |

### Knowledge-graph entity · 4 pts  `p3.knowledge-graph`

A knowledge-base entry is the single strongest signal an engine has for confirming an entity's identity.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | no corresponding entry |
| 4 | an entry exists in Wikidata, Wikipedia, Baidu Baike or similar |

### sameAs complete and resolving · 3 pts  `p3.sameas`

sameAs binds the site to its accounts as one entity. Declaring none scores 0 — it does not leave the denominator.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | not declared, or most do not resolve |
| 2 | declared but some are dead |
| 3 | all resolve and belong to the brand |

### Video and multimodal presence · 3 pts  `p3.video`

Multimodal content reaches answers through a different path than text. It adds, it does not substitute.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | no official video |
| 2 | a channel exists but content is sparse |
| 3 | sustained output, with VideoObject on site |

## Answer Fit · 10 points

### Content shaped for extraction · 4 pts  `p4.answer-shape`

The same content is cited more often when a block of it can be lifted out whole.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | long paragraphs, no hierarchy |
| 2 | subheadings present but paragraphs run long |
| 4 | subheadings, lists and tables with paragraphs of workable length |

### Coverage of the questions people ask · 4 pts  `p4.question-coverage`

Ten questions people in this industry actually ask, against pages that answer them head-on. The gaps are the editorial calendar.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | 0–2 of 10 covered |
| 2 | 3–5 covered |
| 3 | 6–8 covered |
| 4 | 9–10 covered |

### Chinese engine readiness · 2 pts  `p4.cn-engines`

Applies when the site addresses the Chinese market; excluded entirely when it does not.

| Pts | Condition |
|:-:|---|
| 0 | crawling blocked, or filing information absent |
| 1 | crawlable |
| 2 | crawlable with ICP filing and entity information complete |

## Bonus · 6 points (bonus — outside the denominator, caps at +6)

### llms-full.txt · 2 pts  `b.llms-full`

A single aggregated full-text file. A non-root path is allowed as long as it is discoverable from llms.txt or robots.txt.

### ai.txt · 2 pts  `b.ai-txt`

Declares an AI usage policy.

### GEO link tags · 1 pts  `b.geo-link`

Declares retrieval-facing link relations in the head.

### speakable markup · 1 pts  `b.speakable`

Marks the region a voice assistant should read aloud.
