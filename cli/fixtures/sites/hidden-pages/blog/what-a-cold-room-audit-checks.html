What a cold-room audit checks

By Maya Okafor, field engineer · 2026-09-12

An inspector who audits a cold room asks for three things: the temperature record for the period under review, the limits each room was held to, and what was done each time a reading left those limits. A paper log answers the first question badly and the third hardly at all, because a missed entry looks the same as a quiet night.

How do inspectors read the record?

They start with the gaps. A record with an hour missing every night suggests that nobody wrote anything down after closing time, and an inspector will then ask how the site knows the room stayed cold. A continuous record from a sensor closes that question, as long as the readings carry the time they were taken and cannot be edited afterwards.

What counts as a corrective action?

An action is a short note that says who looked at the room, what they found and what they did: moved the stock, called the engineer, or confirmed that the door had been left open during a delivery. Frostline keeps an alert open until such a note is written, so the record and the actions sit side by side when the inspector arrives.
