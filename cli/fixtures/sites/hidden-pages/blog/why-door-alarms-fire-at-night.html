Why door alarms fire at night

By Maya Okafor, field engineer · 2026-09-05

Most alarms that wake someone at three in the morning come from a door, not from a failing compressor. A door that does not close fully lets warm air in slowly, the temperature creeps past the limit an hour later, and by then the last person to use the room has gone home and cannot say what happened.

How can a site tell a door from a fault?

A door leaves a signature: the temperature rises in a smooth curve and falls quickly once the door is shut. A failing compressor shows a slower rise that does not recover, often with short cycles before it. Frostline marks the first pattern as a probable door event, which helps the person on call decide whether to drive in or to ask the night guard to check the handle.

What stops the alarms?

Door closers, strip curtains and a delay of ten or fifteen minutes before an alert is sent remove most of them. The delay matters: a room that is opened for a delivery will always warm briefly, and an alert for every delivery teaches people to ignore the phone.
