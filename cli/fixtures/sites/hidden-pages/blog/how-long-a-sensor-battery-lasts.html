How long a sensor battery lasts

By Maya Okafor, field engineer · 2026-08-28

A Frostline sensor that reports once a minute runs for about three years on its two lithium cells in a chiller, and for a little less in a freezer, where the cold slows the chemistry that delivers the current. The service warns you when a battery falls below a fifth of its charge, which leaves several weeks to replace it.

What shortens battery life?

Three things: a weak radio link, which makes the sensor repeat its messages; a short reporting interval; and repeated warming and cooling, as in a van that is loaded several times a day. A sensor placed behind a metal shelf can use twice the energy of one placed on a wall with a clear path to the gateway.

How do you change a battery?

Open the case with a coin, replace both cells with the same type, and close it again. The sensor keeps its settings and its history, and the gap in the record is the minute or two the case was open.
