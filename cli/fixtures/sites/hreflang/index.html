Kettles that tell you when to descale

Lumen Kettle makes electric kettles with a scale sensor in the base. The kettle measures how long the element takes to bring a litre to the boil, and when that time grows by a fifth it lights a ring on the handle, which is about when limescale starts to cost energy and taste. Each kettle is sold in Germany, France and the United Kingdom, with a manual in each language.

How does the scale sensor work?

The base records the boil time of every full kettle for the first two weeks and keeps that as its reference. Scale insulates the element, so the same litre takes longer as it builds up. The ring lights when the average of the last ten boils is twenty per cent above the reference, and goes out after a descaling cycle brings it back.

What does a kettle cost to run?

A litre boiled from tap temperature takes about a tenth of a kilowatt hour. A family that boils five full kettles a day uses roughly one hundred and eighty kilowatt hours a year on it, and a scaled element can add a tenth to that before anyone notices the slower boil.
