How hard water shortens a kettle's life

By Mara Lindqvist, product engineer. Updated 15 September 2026.

Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium, which settle on the element as limescale each time the water boils. A millimetre of scale can add about a tenth to the energy a boil takes, because the element has to heat the scale before it heats the water. Over a few years the element runs hotter than it was built to, and that is what usually ends a kettle's life.

Why does scale build faster in some homes?

The harder the water, the more mineral each litre leaves behind. A household in a hard-water area that boils a full kettle five times a day can see visible scale within two weeks, while the same kettle in a soft-water area may stay clean for months.

What can a household do about it?

Descaling when the scale is still thin takes a few minutes and a spoonful of citric acid. Waiting until the element is crusted takes several cycles and leaves the kettle drawing more power in the meantime. A filter jug slows the build-up, though it does not stop it.
